\question{Ablation reproducibility: The architectural and feature-subset ablation experiments are reported using a single seed (Seed = 42), whereas the primary evaluation uses five independent seeds. The authors should briefly justify this choice or indicate whether the principal ablation conclusions are stable across multiple seeds.}

\response{We thank the reviewer for raising this important methodological question regarding experimental reproducibility.

\textbf{Methodological Justification for Single-Seed Ablation Setup:}
The architectural component ablation and user-selected feature ablation experiments were deliberately conducted under a standardised single-seed hyperparameter configuration ($\text{Seed} = 42$, $\text{Batch Size} = 256$) to isolate the direct dynamic impact of structural modifications (e.g. disabling individual feature branches, omitting attention gating, or constraining input feature subsets) under strictly identical random weight initialisations, optimiser trajectories, and dataset split partitions across all model variants.

This controlled single-seed setup provides a consistent comparative baseline by holding the random initialisation, optimiser trajectory, and split partition fixed across model variants, thereby reducing the variation attributable to stochastic initialisation when assessing the direct effect of the structural modifications. In contrast, the five-seed evaluation protocol ($S=\{13, 23, 33, 43, 53\}$, batch size $128$) is reserved for evaluating the primary full proposed framework to demonstrate statistical robustness and multi-seed generalisation.

The empirical single-seed ablation results clearly demonstrate the fundamental structural interactions—most notably, that unweighted feature concatenation (\textit{Fusion w/o Attention Gating}) induces severe numerical collapse across multiple dataset tasks due to scale mismatches between CNN and BiLSTM representations (e.g., accuracy dropping to 73.262\% on Apose-IoT-23 binary, 0.489\% on BoT-IoT multiclass, and 2.063\% on CIC-IoMT-2024 binary), whereas attention-based feature gating consistently resolves this degradation and restores optimisation stability.

We have updated Section 6.5 of the main manuscript and Section 8 of the Supplementary Material to explicitly document this methodological justification and clearly distinguish the single-seed ablation protocol from the multi-seed primary evaluation.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Exact Updated Text in Section 6.5 of the Manuscript:}
\begin{quote}
\textit{``\textbf{Ablation Hyperparameter Configuration:} For both the architectural component ablation and user-selected feature ablation experiments, the hyperparameters were standardised with a single seed ($\text{Seed} = 42$, $\text{Batch Size} = 256$, $\text{Epochs} = 20$ with early stopping patience $= 8$, $\text{AdamW}$ optimiser $\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, and $\text{Cross-Entropy}$ loss) to isolate architectural dynamic changes under strictly identical weight initialisations, optimiser trajectories, and split partitions. This fixed single-seed setup is explicitly distinguished from the multi-seed ($S=\{13, 23, 33, 43, 53\}$, batch size $128$) evaluation protocol utilised for the primary model performance benchmarking.''}
\end{quote}
}

\question{Interpretation of ablation results: The conclusions drawn from the ablation results should be stated cautiously. The full attention-gated framework does not achieve the highest accuracy in every individual dataset/task configuration. Therefore, the manuscript should avoid implying that the complete architecture universally outperforms every individual branch and should instead explain the contribution of attention-based gating in the context of the combined CNN–BiLSTM representation.}

\response{We completely agree with the reviewer's insightful assessment. 

As the empirical data in Table 10 show, standalone single branches achieve top accuracy on specific dataset/task configurations; for instance, the BiLSTM branch alone achieves top accuracy on the CIC-IoMT-2024 multiclass (99.218\%) and Apose-IoT-23 multiclass (99.143\%) datasets, whereas the CNN branch alone achieves top accuracy on the CIC-IoT-2025 binary (98.694\%) dataset. 

We have thoroughly revised Section 6.5 of the manuscript and Section 8 of the Supplementary Material to eliminate any implications of the universal accuracy dominance. Instead, we reframe the contribution of attention-based feature gating as dynamic representation integration:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CNN Branch:} Captures spatial / local feature representations.
    \item \textbf{BiLSTM Branch:} Captures sequential / temporal feature representations.
    \item \textbf{Attention-Based Feature Gating:} Dynamically rescales and balances spatial and sequential representations per sample, preventing the scale-mismatch collapse observed in unweighted fusion and enabling effective representation fusion.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Exact Updated Text in Section 6.5 of Manuscript:}
\begin{quote}
\textit{``\textbf{Dynamic Feature Integration via Attention-Based Feature Gating:} The integration of the attention-based feature-gating layer in the \textit{Full Proposed Framework} addresses this scale disparity by learning dynamic, instance-level softmax weight vectors $\alpha = \text{softmax}(e)$. The gating mechanism rescales and balances spatial (CNN) and recurrent (BiLSTM) feature sub-vectors prior to the dense classifier, restoring optimisation stability and eliminating the severe numerical collapse observed under unweighted fusion. The ablation results suggest that attention-based feature gating contributes primarily through selective integration of the complementary representations learned by the CNN and BiLSTM branches. Although the complete framework does not yield the highest accuracy in every dataset/task configuration, the ablation patterns indicate that feature gating can improve the utilisation of complementary spatial and sequential information under several experimental conditions. These results therefore support the role of attention-based gating as a task-dependent integration mechanism rather than as a universally dominant component.''}
\end{quote}
}
